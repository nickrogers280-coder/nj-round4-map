I.       INTRODUCTION

1.1      EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The term ‘master plan’ is defined by the Municipal Land Use Law (“MLUL”) (N.J.S.A. 40:55D-1 et seq.) as ‘a composite of
one or more written or graphic proposals for the development of the municipality.” In accordance with the MLUL, the
preparation and adoption of a master plan is a primary function of the municipality’s Planning Board which is
responsible for establishing the land use policies, goals and objectives intended to achieve the future vision of the
community. The master plan serves as the basis for the municipality’s land use and zoning ordinances which must be
consistent with the provisions of the plan. To encourage consistency between these policy and regulatory documents,
and address any changes in local and broader conditions, the MLUL requires the Planning Board to conduct a periodic
reexamination or analysis of the master plan and development regulations at least once every 10 years.

This 2026 Reexamination Report of the Master Plan and Development Regulations and Amendment to the Township’s
Land Use Plan Element and Housing Element and Fair Share Plan (“HE&FSP”) are intended to address emerging land
use issues and updates to the Township’s Fourth Round affordable housing compliance plan as that process has
evolved. The Planning Board’s specific recommendations and land use policies contained herein are summarized as
follows:

        Implement the terms of the Settlement Agreement with Fair Share Housing Center signed by FSHC on July 7,
         2026, signed by the Township on July 10, 2026 and filed via eCourts on July 13, 2026 attached hereto.

        Review and confirmation of recommendations and land use policies in the 2023 Reexamination Report, 2006
         Land Use Plan Element as amended and 2026 Housing Element and Fair Share Plan.

        Recommendation to place the area of the existing PUD Planned Unit Development Overlay Zone developed
         as the Beaver Brook Country Club and associated townhouse development within a new PUD Planning Unit
         Development Zone that removes the current underlying RR-4S Zone designation which is no longer applicable.

        Recommendation to create a new PUD-AHO Planned Unit Development Affordable Housing Overlay Zone to
         facilitate residential or mixed-use development of Block 79.02 Lots 7, 7.1100 and 7.4100 currently located in the
         PUD Planned Unit Development Overlay Zone.

        Recommendation to remove Block 79.02 Lots 23, 23.01 and 23.02 from the PUD Overlay Zone to remain solely
         in their current underlying RR-4S Zone designations.

        Identify the extension of affordability controls for the 3 existing units at 142 West Main Street and 35 units at
         The Mews as plan mechanisms in the Township’s Fourth Round HE&FSP as amended.

This 2026 Reexamination Report is generally consistent with the land use policies and goals of the Township’s previous
planning documents, particularly its 2023 Reexamination Report, 2006 Land Use Plan Element as amended and 2026
Housing Element and Fair Share Plan. The recommendations set forth in this report are intended to preserve and
enhance the rural character of the community, protect existing residential and environmentally sensitive areas, promote
economic development in appropriate locations and provide affordable housing as detailed herein.

1.2      LEGAL REQUIREMENTS FOR PLANNING

The MLUL establishes the legal requirements and criteria for the preparation of a master plan and reexamination report.
The Planning Board is responsible for the preparation of a master plan and amendments thereto which establish the
land use policies of the municipalities. The master plan gives the community the legal basis to control development in
the municipality through the adoption of development ordinances that are designed to implement the plan’s policies
and recommendations.

The MLUL identifies the required contents of a master plan which include the following:

    1.     A statement of objectives, principals, assumptions, policies and standards upon which the constituent
           proposals for the physical, economic and social development of the municipality are based.

    2.     A land use plan element that takes into account physical features, identifying the existing and proposed
           location, extent and intensity of development for residential and non-residential purposes, and states the
           relationship of the plan to any proposed zone plan and zoning ordinance.

    3.     A housing plan and recycling plan prepared by the municipality.

In addition, the MLUL identifies several other plan elements that may be incorporated into a comprehensive master
plan document, such as: circulation, open space, recreation, community facilities, and historic preservation plan
elements. These elements are optional.

The Planning Board is also responsible for the preparation of a reexamination report of the master plan and
development ordinances which serves as an analysis of the current land use policies and regulations, which may also
include recommended amendments to those documents. The MLUL was amended in May 2011 to require planning
boards to prepare a reexamination report at least once every ten years. Previously, planning boards were required to
prepare such a review at least once every six years.

It is the Clinton Township Planning Board’s intent to amend the Township’s Land Use Plan and Housing Plan Elements
to implement certain recommendations contained in this 2026 Reexamination Report and establish same as land use
policy. In accordance with N.J.S.A. 40:55D-28a, “the planning board may prepare and, after public hearing, adopt or
amend a master plan or component parts thereof, to guide the use of lands within the municipality in a manner which
protects public health and safety and promotes the general welfare. The Township’s master plan amendments
incorporated in this document have been prepared and implemented accordingly.

1.3     LEGAL REQUIREMENTS FOR REEXAMINATION REPORTS

The following section details the statutory requirements of a periodic reexamination report, as prescribed in Section
40:55D-89 of the MLUL. This section of the statute mandates the report must address, at a minimum, the following
components:

   1.      The major problems and objectives relating to land development in the municipality at the time of the
           adoption of the last reexamination report;

   2.      The extent to which such problems and objectives have been reduced or have increased subsequent to
           such date;

   3.      The extent to which there have been significant changes in the assumptions, policies and objectives forming
           the basis for the master plan or development regulations as last revised, with particular regard to the density
           and distribution of population and land use, housing conditions, circulation, conservation of natural
           features, energy conservation, collection, disposition and recycling of designated recyclable materials, and
           changes in State, County and municipal policies and objectives;

   4.      The specific changes recommended for the master plan or development regulations, if any, including
           underlying objectives, policies and standards, or whether a new plan or regulation should be prepared;

   5.      The recommendations of the Planning Board concerning the incorporation of redevelopment plans
           adopted pursuant to the "Local Redevelopment and Housing Law," into the land use plan element of the
           municipal Master Plan, and recommended changes, if any, in the local development regulations necessary
           to effectuate the redevelopment plans of the municipality; and

   6.      The recommendations of the Planning Board concerning locations appropriate for the development
           of public electric vehicle infrastructure, including, but not limited to, commercial districts, areas
           proximate to public transportation and transit facilities and transportation corridors, and public rest
           stops; and recommended changes, if any, in the local development regulations necessary or
           appropriate for the development of public electric vehicle infrastructure.

2.2    PLANNING ISSUES DISCUSSED IN 2023 REEXAMINATION REPORT

  1.     Utilities. As noted in the 2020 Reexamination Report, the availability of water and sewer capacity in the
         Township remains severely constrained. The Township is legally obligated to prioritize the allocation of any
         available water and sewer capacity to the several affordable housing developments as specified in the
         Township’s Third Amendment to its Settlement Agreement with Fair Share Housing Center. Areas of the
         Township reliant on private wells are now subject to more stringent NJDEP and Highlands regulations
         limiting the locations and extent to which water can be extracted from the ground based on a variety of
         environmental constraints and Highlands designations such as ‘water deficit areas.’ Similarly, the locations
         and capacities of on-site septic and treatment facilities are constrained by NJDEP and Highlands regulations.
         The Township has prepared an updated Wastewater Management Plan (“WMP”) which is currently in review
         with the NJDEP.

         2026 Policy Statement. The availability of water and sewer utilities in Clinton Township remains severely
         constrained and is a significant limiting factor to future development in the Township. The Township's
         WMP as a chapter of Hunterdon County's WMP was adopted on February 20, 2026.

3.1     AFFORDABLE HOUSING

On March 20, 2024, Governor Murphy signed Bill A4/S50 into law, which significantly amended the Fair Housing Act
(“FHA”) creating a new framework for determining municipal affordable housing obligations and compliance for the
Fourth Round (2025-2035) and beyond . The amendments to the FHA (commonly referred to as the “FHA-2”) eliminated
the Council on Affordable Housing (“COAH”), kept superior oversight of the trial courts once again responsible for
approving municipal HE&FSPs, and created a new entity to act as an intermediary in resolving disputes and reviewing
municipal plans known as the Affordable Housing Dispute Resolution Program (“the Program“). The Program consists
of retired Mount Laurel Judges with the discretion to hire “Special Adjudicators“ (formerly “Special Masters”) to assist in
their processing of municipal compliance plans and third party challenges to those plans. The FHA-2 also incorporates
the Department of Community Affairs (“DCA”) and the Administrative Office of the Courts (“AOC”) in the process.

The DCA was designated by the FHA-2 as the entity responsible for calculating the State’s and regional affordable
housing needs as well as each municipality’s present and prospective fair share obligations pursuant to the
methodology set forth in the unpublished Jacobson Decision from the Third Round. However, the FHA-2 makes it clear
that these numbers are advisory and non-binding, and that each municipality is required to determine its own obligation
numbers utilizing the same methodology. Significant to Clinton Township, the FHA-2 establishes special consideration
for conforming Highlands municipalities in the calculation of affordable housing obligations and compliance
requirements, which generally must adhere to the Highlands Regional Master Plan and associated Land Use Capability
Zones.

The Program was tasked to process any disputes or challenges to the determination of municipal affordable housing
obligations and compliance plans and issue recommendations to the Superior Court judges who are responsible for
issuing final compliance certifications. Such compliance certification grants municipalities immunity from all exclusionary
zoning lawsuits until the end of the Fourth Round on July 30, 2035.

3.4     CLIMATE CHANGE-RELATED HAZARD VULNERABILITY ASSESSMENT (CCRHVA)

The Municipal Land Use Law (“MLUL”) was amended (N.J.S.A. 40:55D-28b.(2)(h)) by the State Legislature in 2021 to
include a climate change-related hazard vulnerability assessment with the adoption of a municipal land use plan
element of the master plan. These assessments require several components, including: 1) an analysis of current and
future threats and vulnerabilities related to natural hazards in the municipality; 2) build-out analysis that considers such
vulnerabilities; 3) identification of critical facilities and infrastructure; 4) assessment if natural hazards on the master plan;
5) strategies and design standards to address vulnerabilities; 6) policy statement incorporating the CCRHVA into the
municipal master plan; and 7) utilization of the most current natural hazard projections and data from NJDEP. The
Clinton Township Planning Board adopted the Township’s first CCRHVA on August 18, 2025 fulfilling this statutory
requirement. That document is available on the Township’s website.

     3. Climate Change Hazard Vulnerability Assessment

        The Municipal Land Use Law (“MLUL”) was amended (N.J.S.A. 40:55D-28b.(2)(h)) by the State Legislature in
        2021 to include a climate change-related hazard vulnerability assessment with the adoption of a municipal land
        use plan element of the master plan. These assessments require several components, including: 1) an analysis
        of current and future threats and vulnerabilities related to natural hazards in the municipality; 2) build-out
        analysis that considers such vulnerabilities; 3) identification of critical facilities and infrastructure; 4) assessment
        if natural hazards on the master plan; 5) strategies and design standards to address vulnerabilities; 6) policy
        statement incorporating the CCRHVA into the municipal master plan; and 7) utilization of the most current
        natural hazard projections and data from NJDEP. The Clinton Township Planning Board in collaboration with
        the Township’s emergency response personnel and DPW adopted the Township’s first CCRHVA on August 18,
        2025 fulfilling this statutory requirement. The Township’s CCRHVA shall be utilized to inform the Township’s
        land use decision making and capital expenditures.

        The Township’s 2025 Climate Change-Related Hazard Vulnerability Assessment is available on the Township
        website.

2. Extension of Affordability Controls

   The Township will make reasonable efforts to extend the affordability controls of the 3 existing affordable units
   at 142 West Main Street that will expire in 2031 and 35 existing senior affordable housing units at The Mews
   that will expire from November 20, 2032 to December 3, 2034 in accordance with the Township’s Fourth Round
   Settlement Agreement with FSHC. The Township will not be required to pay more than $25,000 per extension
   of controls on a unit, the payments of which shall be made from the Township’s Affordable Housing Trust Fund,
   with no payments having to be made out of the Township’s general funds. The $25,000 cap shall include the
   costs for repairs to a unit to enable it to obtain a certificate of continued occupancy and/or to bring the unit up
   to code.

   The extensions of controls for the 35 units in the Mews would be a first extension for those units. The extension
   of controls for the 3 units located at 142 West Main Street would be a second extension for those units. The
   original deed restrictions for the 142 West Main Street units were recorded in 1994 and set to expire on
   December 31, 2024 but were extended by the Township in 2001 and will expire on April 1, 2031. The parties
   recognize that while the Township completed an extension of controls in 2001 that led to these units expiring
   in 2031, the Township has never received any credit for extending controls beyond the original thirty (30) year
   deed restriction and thus, these units are eligible for extension of control credit now. The Township will seek to
   extend the period of controls of the units at these two sites for a 30-year period.

   It is understood that if the owner(s) / landlord of any or all units do not agree to extend the controls after being
   offered reasonable financial compensation as defined above, the Township shall not be deemed to be in
   violation of its Fourth Round Settlement Agreement with FSHC.

3. Challenges to Fourth Round HE&FSP

   The Township’s Fourth Round Fair Share Plan as amended addresses its various affordable housing obligations
   with a variety of mechanisms that conform with the Highlands RMP. In accordance with the FHA-2 and
   applicable COAH regulations, the Township is entitled to select development sites and plan mechanisms
   deemed most appropriate for the community in satisfying its affordable housing obligations. Nevertheless, the
   Township received three challenges to its June 2025 HE&FSP pursuant to the FHA-2 mediation process that
   were evaluated through the Affordable Housing Dispute Resolution Program.

   To date, the Township successfully reached a settlement agreement with FSHC last signed by the Township on
   July 10, 2026, but has not resolved the challenges to its Fourth Round HE&FSP from CNC Ventures (Beaver
   Brook Country Club) and ExxonMobil. The reasons stated in Section 4.4 of the Township’s March 2026 HE&FSP
   as to why the ExxonMobil and CNC Ventures sites are not included in the Township’s Fair Housing Plan remain
   valid.
