INTRODUCTION

This document has been prepared as an amendment to Cranbury Township’s Fourth Round
Housing Element and Fair Share Plan (“HEFSP”), which was previously adopted by the
Township Planning Board on June 24, 2025, endorsed by the Township Committee on June
26, 2025 and submitted to the Superior Court and the Affordable Housing Dispute Resolution
Program (“Program”) on June 26, 2025 pursuant to the amended Fair Housing Act (“FHA”)
at N.J.S.A. 52:27D-301 et seq.

Subsequently, several interested parties filed challenges to the Township’s HEFSP in
accordance with N.J.S.A. 52:27D-304.1.f(2)(b). The challengers included Fair Share Housing
Center (“FSHC”), Henry Realty Company, LLC (“HRC”), Cranbury Housing Associates
(“CHA”), and Axria, LLC and Axria’s entity Onyx Realty, LLC (“Axria/Onyx”). Thereafter, two
Program mediation settlement conferences and a Program session were held before the
Honorable Mary C. Jacobson, A.J.S.C. (ret.) and Special Adjudicator David Banisch, PP, AICP,
and the parties.

The Township, FSHC, CHA and HRC entered into various settlement discussions. Regarding
the challenge from Axria/Onyx, the Township filed responses to the Program Judge and
Special Adjudicator setting forth substantive reasons for the Axria/Onyx challenge to be
dismissed.

During the Program mediation conferences, agreements were reached including the
December 29, 2025 Fourth Round Mediation Agreement, between Cranbury Township and
FSHC (included as Attachment 1), a 2026 Memorandum of Understanding executed between
the Township and CHA regarding the CHA at Cranbury Commons site (Attachment 2), the
terms of a pending settlement agreement between the Township and CHA regarding a
proposed extensions of controls program and a group home program, as embodied in a
December 26, 2025 letter prepared by Kevin J. Moore, Esq., CHA’s attorney (Attachment 3)
and, per a letter, dated December 31, 2025 from Township attorney Kevin Van Hise to Program
Judge Jacobson outlining the terms whereby HRC agreed to dismiss its challenge pending the
Court’s approval of the replacement of the Henry Farm site as reflected in the December 29,
2025 executed FSHC agreement and in this 2026 HEFSP amendment (Attachment 4).

Pursuant to N.J.S.A. 52:27D-304.1.f(2)(b) and Administrative Directive #14-24, if the various
agreements are approved by the Superior Court, such action will result in a judgment of
repose/compliance certification for the Township for the Fourth Round through June 30,
2035.

On February 5, 2026, Program Judge Jacobson issued the Program Decision and
Recommendations report, finding that the terms of the settlements reached by the Township
were fair, reasonable and adequately protect the interests of low and moderate-income
residents of the Township, that the settlements are constitutionally compliant and provide a
fair and reasonable opportunity for the Township to meet its obligations under the FHA and
the Mount Laurel doctrine, and recommending that: the terms of the settlement agreements
be approved; the challenge by Onyx be dismissed; the Township adopt and file its amended

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
…
The capped Fourth Round Prospective obligation (2025-2035) of 265 will be satisfied through
the following mechanisms: 10 surplus Third Round credits from Birchwood at Cranbury (2
bal. of 5 special needs rentals), Cranbury Commons inclusionary development (1 bal. of 7
family rentals), an existing Eden/CHA group home at 23 Old Cranbury Road (3 bedrooms),
and a proposed Eden/CHA group home at 19 Old Cranbury Road (4 bedrooms); 190 units
from two proposed 100% affordable housing developments at 1260 South River Road (two
phases w/ 72 and 58 family rentals)and 1274 South River Road (55 senior and 5 special needs
rentals); and 66 Fourth Round bonuses. In addition to these Fourth Round mechanisms, the
Township will support CHA in developing a 100% affordable 24-unit family rental
development; will seek extensions of controls on 26 of CHA’s existing affordable family
rentals; and will support the development of two group homes to be operated by Eden Autism
Services (“EAS”).

CRANBURY TOWNSHIP’S AFFORDABLE HOUSING HISTORY
…
The Township prepared a 2025 Fourth Round HEFSP that was adopted by the Planning Board
on June 24, 2025, endorsed by the Township and submitted to the Superior Court and
Program on June 26, 2025. As previously discussed, several interested parties including
FSHC, CHA, Axria/Onyx and HRC subsequently filed challenges to the 2025 HEFSP by the
statutory deadline. The Township and the challenging parties participated in several Program
mediation settlement conferences and a Program session held before Judge Jacobson and
Special Adjudicator David Banisch.

The Township has since reached agreements with three of the four parties, including a
December 29, 2025 Fourth Round Mediation Agreement with FSHC (Attachment 1), a 2026
MOU executed with CHA regarding the CHA at Cranbury Commons site (Attachment 2), and
a pending settlement agreement with CHA regarding a proposed extensions of controls
program and a group home program (Attachment 3). HRC has also agreed to dismiss its
challenge pending Court approval of the 1260 South River Road replacement site (Attachment
4)

On February 5, 2026, Program Judge Jacobson issued a Decision and Recommendation
determining that the settlement agreements are constitutionally compliant, recommending
dismissal of the Axria/Onyx challenge, and recommending the issuance of a Certification of
Compliance and Repose (Attachment 5). On February 9, 2026, Judge McCloskey accepted the
Program’s recommendations, dismissed the Axria/Onyx challenge, and authorized Cranbury
Township to proceed with adoption of this HEFSP Amendment and implementing
ordinances and resolutions by March 16, 2026 (Attachment 6).

Page 24-26 of 2025 Cranbury Fourth Round HEFSP:

CONSIDERATION OF LAND FOR AFFORDABLE HOUSING
…
The Township believes that the proposed developments indicated in this document represent
the best options for affordable development within the Township. As documented in this
HEFSP amendment, the Township has proposed three 100% affordable housing
developments located at 1260 South River Road, 1274 South River Road, and at the corner of
Old Trenton Road and South Main Street (the remaining one (1) acre parcel of the existing
Cranbury Commons development). The proposed South River Road sites are served by public
transportation and all sites have access to significant job opportunities as well as the historical
Village of Cranbury, thus permitting very-low-, low- and moderate-income households to be a
part of the community with access to local schools, parks and services.

1260 South River Road – Family Affordable Rentals (Proposed)

The Township intends to address its Fourth Round obligation through a proposed 100%
affordable family rental development in partnership with The Walters Group (“Walters
Group”). The proposed affordable housing site is an 11.82-acre parcel (Block 5, Lot 18) located
on the easterly side of South River Road, across from its intersection with Dey Road (see overall
aerial site map below). The site is primarily agricultural land, with the southern portion
developed and occupied by the PDM Group, an HVAC equipment services company. Existing
improvements include a two-story residence, a detached garage, a one-story building used for
equipment storage, and a stormwater management basin. There is a current site plan approval
from the Cranbury Township Planning Board for a non-residential development on the site.
As discussed below, the development approvals include NJ Department of Environmental
Protection (“DEP”) approvals for wetlands/related buffers and flood hazard areas.

At this time, the Walters Group anticipates the site will be developed in two (2) phases with
the first phase consisting of 72 affordable units and a second phase of 58 affordable units.
Together, both phases will total 130 family affordable rental units located within approximately
six (6) multifamily buildings plus community amenities such as a shared community building
and proposed active recreation/passive open space, as shown on the preliminary December
18, 2025 concept plan (see below) provided by the Walters Group . As summarized in the table
below, the development will include 18 very low-income units, 48 low-income units, and 64
moderate-income units.
